MEETING DESIGN

A small Dallas meeting with room to think

One clear outcome, a realistic agenda, and a room that supports the conversation.

MEETINGS & EVENTS DALLAS · OCTOBER 6, 2026

Define what should change by the end

Before choosing a meeting room, finish this sentence: by the end, our group should be able to do or decide something specific. If that answer is unclear, work on it before reserving more hours.

Separate updates people can read beforehand from questions that benefit from a live conversation. Send the background early and use the room for discussion.

Design the agenda around attention

For a half-day session, consider an opening discussion, a focused working block, a break, and a closing decision. These are suggested building blocks, not a mandatory schedule. Adjust them to the work and the people in the room.

Leave a small buffer before the start for arrivals. Protect the final stretch for decisions, owners, and next steps; otherwise the most useful part of the day can get squeezed out.

Choose a room you can use

Ask the venue about seating arrangements, screen visibility, lighting controls, power access, and accessibility. A room that works for a lecture may need a different arrangement for group work.

For hybrid attendance, confirm who supplies microphones and how remote participants will hear discussions away from the presenter. Test the actual setup before guests arrive.

Close with a shared record

Before leaving, agree on a short record of the decisions and who owns each next action. Give people time to correct misunderstandings while everyone is still together.

If you want a meal afterward, make it a separate, optional plan. A productive meeting does not have to become an all-day commitment for every participant.
